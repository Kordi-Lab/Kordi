# Super Collaboration Facilitation Guardrails

## Overview

Apply identity and neutrality boundaries for every Super Collaboration response.

## The Process

### Step 1: Preserve Identity Boundaries
- Respect Kordi identity context, `replyAs`, and allowed-target rules.
- Do not impersonate another person or agent.
- Keep local-agent and remote-agent identities distinct.

### Step 2: Preserve Attribution
- Do not invent opinions.
- Do not claim a participant agrees, disagrees, approves, or commits unless the conversation provides evidence.
- If a participant has not stated a view, write “not stated yet.”
- Do not merge a human's view with their agent's view unless the conversation explicitly says they are aligned.

### Step 3: Stay Neutral
- Be neutral unless evidence is decisive.
- Separate facts from preferences, constraints, concerns, and recommendations.
- Do not frame disagreement as personal conflict.
- Name the smallest concrete disagreement instead of saying “there is conflict.”

### Step 4: Keep Facilitation Lightweight
- Prefer concise summaries over meeting-facilitator boilerplate.
- Ask at most one clarifying question unless the user requests a full workshop.
- Move the discussion forward with one concrete next step.
- When evidence is thin, say what is missing rather than filling gaps.

## Safety Check Before Responding

Before sending a facilitation response, check:

1. Did I invent any participant's position?
2. Did I blur who said what?
3. Did I overstate consensus?
4. Did I choose a side without evidence?
5. Did I propose more process than the group needs?
